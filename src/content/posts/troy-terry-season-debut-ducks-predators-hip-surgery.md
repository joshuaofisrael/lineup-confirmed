Anaheim Ducks forward Troy Terry made his season debut against the Nashville Predators on Wednesday, [NHL.com's Status Report said](https://www.nhl.com/news/nhl-status-report-news-and-notes-november-11-2026). He had not played this season after surgery on June 9 to repair a hip impingement and a labral tear, with an expected recovery time of five to six months.

"This is the next step for me," Terry said before the game, per NHL.com.

## How the return went

The Ducks lost 4-2 at Honda Center. Terry played 11:41 with no shots and was minus-3, according to the [NHL.com recap](https://www.nhl.com/news/nashville-predators-anaheim-ducks-game-recap-november-11-2026). He said he spent much of the night getting a feel for his game and his body, and felt better as it went on.

Before the game, coach Joel Quenneville said Terry would likely start on the third line with Tim Washe, [the Ducks' preview said](https://www.nhl.com/ducks/news/preview-ducks-seek-sixth-straight-win-as-terry-returns-to-lineup-against-predators). Quenneville described him as a dependable winger who can play the power play and penalty kill.

Terry was limited to 61 games last season by the hip injury and had 57 points (19 goals, 38 assists), per NHL.com.

## Goalies and the rest of the game

- **Ducks:** Laurent Brossoit started and made 18 saves. He had won his first four starts for Anaheim, per the team preview.
- **Predators:** Justus Annunen made 27 saves on the second night of a back-to-back, a night after Juuse Saros made 30 in a 3-0 win at Los Angeles.

Nashville (10-4-5) has won six in a row for the first time since March 2024. Cutter Gauthier scored for Anaheim (10-8-1) for the seventh straight game, giving him 14 goals in that span. NHL.com said he is the first player to do that since Mario Lemieux and Alexander Mogilny in 1992-93.

Anaheim had won five straight before Wednesday. Earlier this month we covered [Brossoit's shutout over Pittsburgh](/articles/brossoit-4-0-ducks-beat-penguins-gauthier-hat-trick/).
