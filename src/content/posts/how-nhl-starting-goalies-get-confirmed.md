Every NHL team dresses two goalies for a game. Rule 5.1 of the [2026-27 official rules](https://media.nhl.com/site/asset/public/ext/2026-27/2026-27Rules.pdf) says a team is made up of 20 players, 18 skaters and two goalkeepers. Rule 5.3 says one goalkeeper is on the ice at a time, and the substitute is on the bench or on a chair immediately beside it, fully equipped and ready to play. The open question on a game night is which of the two starts.

This desk sorts every answer into three labels on the [NHL starting goalie board](/nhl/starting-goalies/). These are our labels, not league terms.

## Confirmed

A goalie is confirmed when the team, the league, or a reporter at the rink says he starts, or when the box score shows he did. Coaches often name the starter after the morning skate. Some teams post it on their own channels. When that word is public, the board says so and links where it came from.

## Expected

Expected is a projection. The most common basis is a morning-skate note, such as a goalie being first off the ice, which beat reporters often read as a hint about the start. It is a strong signal but not an announcement, and coaches can and do change plans. Our [morning skate explainer](/articles/nhl-morning-skate-explained-starting-goalie/) covers how to read it.

## Unconfirmed

Unconfirmed means we do not yet have a public word either way. It is not a guess that the backup will play.

## The last checkpoint before puck drop

The rulebook sets the lineup deadline, and it is later than the morning skate. Under Rule 5.1 of the [2026-27 official rules](https://media.nhl.com/site/asset/public/ext/2026-27/2026-27Rules.pdf), the visiting club's manager or coach submits the list of eligible players in the NHL Game Line-up Management app within five minutes of the end of warm-up, which is 20 minutes before face-off. If the app will not connect, the same person hands in the paper Club Playing Roster form, in that same window. After the game starts, that list does not change.

Rule 7.1 is the starting lineup. The visiting club names it with that submission. The home club names its starting lineup after it has been told the visitor's. A change after the names are in needs the referee's approval before the game starts. Our labels do not wait for Rule 7.1 when a coach, the league, or a reporter at the rink has already said who starts. They also do not treat warm-up as the announcement.

Rule 5.3 still allows one goalkeeper on the ice at a time, with the other on the bench or on a chair immediately beside it, fully equipped and ready to play. The [2026-27 EBUG rule](/articles/nhl-emergency-backup-goalie-ebug-rule-explained/) is a roster spot on top of that. It does not name the starter.

Back-to-backs add a wrinkle, because the second night is a separate decision. Our [back-to-back explainer](/articles/nhl-back-to-back-starting-goalie-what-we-confirm/) walks through recent examples. Confirmed starts from the nightly board, limited to NHL.com and club sources, are in the [starting goalie history file](/nhl/starting-goalies/history/). A later team confirmation, on a night the board does not yet archive, is the [Nov. 19 Flyers report](/articles/flyers-joseph-woll-vs-senators-samuel-ersson-starting-goalies-nov-19-2026/).
