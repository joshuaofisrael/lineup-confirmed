Sources & Ethics

These are the desk’s publishing rules. They are our own standards, not legal advice and not a promise about any particular dispute. The disclaimer says what the reporting is not. Corrections says how to fix a fact.

Sources

Every factual claim in a published article cites a named, linkable source in the sentence it supports. The source should be a primary document or a reputable publisher: a team, a league, an on-site beat reporter, or the outlet that reported the fact. The sources list at the bottom repeats those pages with a title, a URL, a publisher, and a date.

We do not invent quotes, numbers, prices, dates, or events. If a figure is not in a source, it stays out. Wording is our own. A quote from a source is brief, and it is attributed.

People and companies

We do not state or imply that a named person or company did something wrong unless an authoritative source says so. When that source says so, the article attributes it, with words such as “according to” or “alleged,” and it links the source. We do not speculate about a person’s motive, health, finances, or private conduct. When a passage is in doubt, we leave it out.

Opinion, analysis, and speculation

News is sourced fact. Opinion, analysis, and speculation are labeled at the top of the article. A label is not a substitute for the sources under the facts inside the piece.

Images

An AI-generated image does not show an identifiable real person and does not depict a fabricated news event. Its caption is exactly “AI-generated image,” and the alt text describes the photograph. A third-party press image is small, credited, and used as fair use. It is not a stock-agency file. Icons and other interface graphics are not article photographs.

Advice

Articles are not betting, fantasy, health, legal, or financial advice. Where a story touches health, law, or money, it carries a short disclaimer and still cites the underlying source. Confirm anything that matters with that source or with a qualified professional.

Corrections and contact

A published article shows a Last updated date. That date is updatedAt in the file, and it is dateModified in the page’s structured data. Send a correction through the contact page. We do not publish a street address.
