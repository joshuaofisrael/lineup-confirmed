What is an NHL EBUG in 2026-27? A traveling goalie on the roster, in No. 00

By Staff Desk

Analysis

For 2026-27 every NHL club must carry an emergency backup who travels, practices and dresses in No. 00. Here is who qualifies.

Last updated November 21, 2026

For 2026-27, an EBUG is an emergency backup goalie the club must carry on its roster. NHL.com's Oct. 2, 2026 list says each team is required to have one, that he dresses with the team, wears No. 00, and that he practices and travels with the club. That is a roster job. It is not, by itself, a starting assignment.

Who qualifies, in the league's Oct. 2 account

The same NHL.com story gives three tests. The goalie must not have played in an NHL game under a standard player contract. He must not have played in more than 80 professional games. He must not have played professional hockey in the previous three seasons.

Those tests leave room for a short professional past. The Oct. 2 entry for the Kings names Sean Maguire and says he was a 2012 draft pick who played six AHL games and never an NHL game. The page is a roll of the goalies clubs had announced, one name per team. A later transaction can change a name. This desk re-read that page on Nov. 21, 2026, and did not re-check all 32 names against a newer club announcement. Use the league list, then the club's own site, before treating one name as current.

What the Oct. 16 story adds

Kevin Woodley's NHL.com story on Oct. 16, 2026 attributes the roster requirement to the new collective bargaining agreement. It repeats the three tests and the No. 00 sweater. It also says the pads have to match the league's equipment-sizing charts, and that they are sent to Kay Whitmore in hockey operations at the league offices in Toronto to be checked against the goalie's leg measurements.

Woodley describes two uses clubs were discussing. Some would keep the extra goalie for the night a call-up cannot arrive in time. Others would use him in practice so the two regulars can skip extra reps. That is workload, not a rule about who starts the game. This page does not turn it into advice about rest or starts.

A club's extra conditions

On Oct. 9, 2026, the Blue Jackets said Reid Robertson, their manager of travel and logistics, would be the club's EBUG. He had already been an unofficial practice goalie. The club's account of eligibility adds lines the Oct. 2 list does not spell out in the same sentence: he travels for home and road games, he is not on an NHL club's reserve list, and he has no other contract that would block the emergency job. The three tests from Oct. 2 are in that club story as well.

Robertson's path is one club's. It is not a template for the other 31 names on the league list.

Rule 5.3 is the goalie already on the bench

The playing rules still describe a different person. Rule 5.3 of the 2026-27 official rules, re-read Nov. 21, 2026, allows one goalkeeper on the ice at a time. Each team keeps a substitute goalkeeper on the bench, or on a chair immediately beside the bench, fully equipped and ready to play. If both listed goalkeepers are incapacitated in a regular-season or playoff game, the team may dress and play any goalkeeper who is eligible. When the two regulars are incapacitated in quick succession, that third goalkeeper gets a reasonable time to dress plus a two-minute warm-up, except when he is already dressed and on the bench, in which case he goes in immediately and the warm-up is not given. The same exception applies when he enters to face a penalty shot.

The roster EBUG can be that third goalkeeper. The rule does not say he is the starter when everyone is healthy.

What this desk does with it

On the NHL starting goalie board, confirmed means a team, the league, or a reporter at the rink said he starts, or the box score shows he did. An EBUG on the roster does not move either of the two regulars from expected to confirmed. The confirmation explainer is the full set of labels. Confirmed starts whose source is an NHL.com or club page are archived in the starting goalie history file.

An earlier version of this page described a local amateur list and treated "never a pro" as the test. The Oct. 2 and Oct. 16 league stories describe the 2026-27 roster rule above. That is the version this page now follows.
