Poland 1-1 Sweden: Sweden's confirmed XI with debuts for Törnqvist and Njie and Gyökeres as captain

By Staff Desk

Graham Potter rotated after Saturday's group-clinching win, starting goalkeeper Noel Törnqvist and winger Alieu Njie for the first time. Lindelöf began on the bench, Nygren was injured, and Hugo Larsson cancelled out Adrian Benedyczak's opener.

Last updated November 18, 2026

Sweden drew 1-1 away to Poland on Tuesday 17 November in their final Nations League group match, the Swedish FA reports. Adrian Benedyczak gave Poland the lead after about a quarter of an hour, and Hugo Larsson equalised less than ten minutes later. Sweden had already won their group and promotion to League A with Saturday's win over Bosnia and Herzegovina.

Sweden starting XI

As listed by EM-fotboll.se: Noel Törnqvist; Felix Beijmo, Gustaf Lagerbielke, Eric Smith, Elliot Stroud; Alexander Bernhardsson, Hugo Larsson, Jesper Karlström, Alieu Njie; Viktor Gyökeres (captain), Yasin Ayari.

Substitutes: Jacob Widell Zetterström, Viktor Johansson, Victor Nilsson Lindelöf, Gabriel Gudmundsson, Daniel Svensson, Alexander Isak, Anthony Elanga, Hjalmar Ekdal, Genesis Antwi, Sebastian Nanasi, Dejan Kulusevski.

The selection news

Head coach Graham Potter made several changes from Saturday, the Swedish FA said before kick-off, and handed first caps to goalkeeper Noel Törnqvist and Fiorentina winger Alieu Njie, who started on the left. "With short time between matches we think it is good to rotate the squad," Potter said (our translation from Swedish).

Victor Nilsson Lindelöf started on the bench, so Viktor Gyökeres wore the captain's armband. Benjamin Nygren was unavailable with a minor injury.

In-game changes

The Swedish FA says Lindelöf came on at half-time and steadied the back line, and Kulusevski and Elanga came on just after the hour. A third debutant, Genesis Antwi, also got on the pitch. Njie set up the equaliser, beating two Poland players before Gyökeres helped the ball on for Larsson to score.

What Potter said

"I think we had a few more chances and could have won the match," Potter told Viaplay after the game, in comments reported by the Swedish FA (our translation). "1-1 away from home is what it is. We'll take it, but a little disappointed that we didn't win."
