NHL Morning Skate Explained: What It Says About the Starting Goalie

By Staff Desk

Analysis

The morning skate is where most starting goalie hints appear. Here is what it is, what reporters watch for, and why we label those hints expected, not confirmed.

Last updated October 31, 2026

On a game day, many NHL teams hold a short, optional practice in the late morning, usually called the morning skate. It is a light session, not a full practice, and some teams skip it, especially on the second night of a back-to-back or after travel.

For anyone tracking goalies, it is the first useful look of the day.

What reporters watch for

The hint most people quote is which goalie leaves the ice first. By long-standing habit on many teams, the projected starter heads off early while the backup stays out for extra work. Beat reporters at the rink post that observation, and coaches often confirm the starter when they speak to media afterwards.

Neither is guaranteed. Habits differ by team and by goalie, and a plan can change after the skate because of illness, injury or a coach's call.

How we label it

On our NHL starting goalie board, a morning-skate observation makes a goalie expected. A coach or team saying he starts makes him confirmed. Our confirmation explainer sets out the labels in full.

What happens after the skate

Both goalies still dress. Rule 5.3 of the NHL rulebook requires the second goalie to be on or beside the bench, fully equipped and ready to play. Rule 5.1 has the visiting team submit its eligible players within five minutes of the end of warm-up, 20 minutes before face-off. If no word has come by then, the warm-up is usually the next clue.
